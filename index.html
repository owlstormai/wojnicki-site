House
rules

The same five rules show up in everything I ship, from a weekend demo to a client deployment. They're the reason the demos survive contact with real users.

rule.cite_or_abstain

Cite it or say "I don't know"

An answer without a source is a guess. A system that admits a gap is one you can deploy.

rule.evals_gate_ci

Evals block the merge

Quality is a failing test, not a feeling after a demo. Regressions stop at the pull request.

rule.by_construction

Safe by construction

Isolation and permissions are built into the shape of the API, then verified by a machine.

rule.seeded_truth

Know the right answer

Seeded synthetic data means the test suite knows ground truth, so correctness is checkable.

rule.show_first

A demo you can click

If I can't put it in front of you running, it isn't done yet.

HemoCheck
killed, not shipped

  1. The idea. Estimate hemoglobin from a phone photo of the nailbed. The prototype worked end to end on iOS.
  2. The wall. Existing patents already cover nailbed-based estimation. That's real IP, not a technicality.
  3. The pivot. I tried the inner eyelid instead. Accuracy didn't hold, and there was no labeled clinical dataset to fix it honestly.
  4. The call. Stop. It stays a prototype until there's a clinical partner and a clear IP path.
You can't ship a medical screening tool on vibes.
